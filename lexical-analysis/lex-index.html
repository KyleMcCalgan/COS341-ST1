From a regex to a working lexer

One pipeline, four stages: write a regular expression, turn it into an NFA, turn that into a DFA, then minimize it. Stack several of these together with priorities and you have a lexer. This section covers all of it — not just the three algorithms (regex→NFA, NFA→DFA, minimization) but everything the textbook builds around them: the theory of what regular languages even are, and the practical mess of turning a DFA into something that actually tokenizes real source code.

Running example throughout: the regular expression (a|b)*ac — small enough to hand-trace fully, and used for the same reason Grammar 2.9 anchors the parser notes: it's the textbook's own worked example, so the NFA, the DFA, and the minimization all chain together off one automaton (8 NFA states → 4 DFA states → 3 minimal states) instead of three disconnected ones.
